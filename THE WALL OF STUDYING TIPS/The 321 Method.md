## Step 1: The Three Reads  
- Instead of reading once and highlighting, read the material exactly three times. 
- The first read is a quick overview to understand the structure (like a pilot's flight map). 
- The second and third reads are for deep dives to identify specific details, proper nouns, and connections.
  
```ad-note
Crucial rule: Absolutely no highlighting is allowed during this phase to avoid the "illusion of fluency."
```

## Step 2: Explain Out Loud
- After reading, close your materials and explain the topic out loud to an empty wall as if teaching someone else.
- This process, known as Bay Song in Chinese tradition, forces your brain to organize information without visual crutches. 
- When you stutter or hit a mental wall, you have identified your true knowledge gaps.
  


## Step 3: The Blank Page Technique 
- The final, most "painful" but essential step: grab a blank piece of paper and write down everything you know about the topic from scratch.
- Writing forces you to slow down and prevents your brain from using "filler words" to fake confidence.
- Afterward, compare your writing to the source material to mark what you missed (in red) and what you got right (in green), creating a targeted map for your future review sessions.

